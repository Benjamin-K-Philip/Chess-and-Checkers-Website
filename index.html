How to play

Chess

The goal: trap the enemy king so that it is attacked and cannot escape. That's checkmate, and it ends the game immediately.

How the pieces move

♔
King · one square, any direction
♕
Queen · any distance, any line
♖
Rook · straight lines only
♗
Bishop · diagonals only
♘
Knight · L-shape, jumps over
♙
Pawn · forward, captures diagonally

Every piece except the knight is blocked by anything in its path. You capture by landing on an enemy piece and removing it.

The three special moves

  • Castling. If neither your king nor a rook has moved and the squares between them are empty, the king slides two squares toward that rook and the rook hops to its other side. You can't castle out of, through, or into check.
  • En passant. If an enemy pawn uses its two-square first move to land beside your pawn, you may capture it as if it had only moved one — but only on the very next turn.
  • Promotion. A pawn reaching the far end becomes any piece you choose. Almost always a queen.

Check, checkmate and draws

When your king is attacked you are in check and must deal with it: move the king, block the line, or capture the attacker. If no legal move exists and you're in check, that's checkmate. If no legal move exists and you're not in check, the game is a stalemate — a draw.

White always moves first. Tap one of your pieces and every legal destination lights up: a dot for a quiet move, a ring for a capture.

How to play

Checkers

The goal: take all of your opponent's discs, or leave them with no legal move. Either one wins the game.

The board and the pieces

Only the dark squares are used, so everything happens on the diagonals. Each side starts with twelve discs on the three rows nearest them. Red always moves first, and whichever colour you pick is drawn at the bottom of the board, moving upward.

Moving and jumping

  • A plain disc moves one square diagonally forward, into an empty square.
  • To capture, jump diagonally over an adjacent enemy disc and land on the empty square directly behind it. The jumped disc is removed.
  • Jumps are compulsory. If a capture is available anywhere, you must play a capture — the game will only offer you those moves.
  • Chain jumps. If the same disc can jump again from its landing square, it must keep going. A four-piece chain in one turn is entirely legal, and the whole chain counts as a single move.

Kings

A disc that reaches the far row is crowned and becomes a king, marked with a crown. Kings move and jump diagonally in all four directions, forward and back. If a disc is crowned in the middle of a jump chain, the turn ends there — it cannot use its new powers until next turn.

The squares are numbered 1–32, top-left to bottom-right, exactly as they are in written checkers. A move reads 11-15; a capture reads 23x14.
